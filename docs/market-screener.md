# Market network and experimental screens

The home network has 50 **discovery candidates** across ten business themes. They are not 50 recommendations. The candidates were checked against the Nasdaq Trader symbol directory dated 2026-09-29, which has 7,124 entries classified as securities in this snapshot. `data/universe.json` names the hand-selected set and its source. `data/us_symbols.json` supplies their current listing names and exchanges and powers broader U.S. ticker search. The theme and `scale` fields are editorial grouping labels. They do not claim an official sector classification or measured market capitalization.

`GET /api/screener` returns the same 50 candidates even without market-data credentials. `ranked` and `picks` are empty until a server-side Alpaca Market Data key is configured and the service receives fresh snapshots. `ALPACA_API_KEY_ID` and `ALPACA_API_SECRET_KEY` (or Alpaca's `APCA_` aliases) are read only on the server. `ALPACA_STOCK_DATA_FEED` may be `iex` or `sip`; `iex` sees one exchange and `sip` requires the relevant subscription. The endpoint fetches all 50 snapshots in one batch, shares an in-process result for 60 seconds, and sets `Cache-Control: no-store` on its response. It does not place orders.

The experimental intraday screen uses each snapshot's current minute bar, latest bid and ask, current daily range, and previous daily close. It excludes missing, inconsistent, delayed, or over-three-minute-old minute/quote data. The underlying signal blocks directional flags when minute dollar volume is under $50,000, quoted spread exceeds 25 basis points, or share price is under $1. A `buy` flag requires all three upward observations and a retrospective long move of at least 0.1% after crossing the quoted spread and assuming 10 basis points slippage on each side. A `sell` flag applies symmetric downward observations and short cost sensitivity. The `score` is an unvalidated directional screen value; it is not expected return, a probability of profit, or account P&L. Fees, taxes, actual fills, and market impact are not included. See `api/signal.js` for exact thresholds.

`ranked` contains only candidates with fresh, complete snapshots. `buy` and `sell` screens both rank ahead of `watch`, so a bearish signal cannot disappear beneath a long list of watches. Within the directional screens, distance from neutral score 50 and the observed move after assumed costs determine order; the absolute minute move breaks remaining ties. A score near 5 is a strong **bearish direction**, not low confidence. `picks` retains at most 12 `buy` screens and at most two from each theme for consumers that need a long-only list. A screen may return zero picks. Outside the [NYSE regular equity session](https://www.nyse.com/trade/hours-calendars), including its published 2026–2028 holidays and early closes, the API returns `status: closed` with empty `ranked` and `picks`, even if extended-hours prices are fresh. If a feed is sparse or data is unavailable, it reports the condition rather than retaining old recommendations. The product should show the source, timestamp, feed, and reason with every screen.

Each ranked row includes `validUntil`, capped by session close and that row's bar/quote freshness. The response's top-level `validUntil` is the earliest ranked expiry. The client should remove expired rows immediately; the server also refreshes its in-process result if this deadline arrives before the usual 60-second refresh.

This is **not** a validated alpha model. Before treating any screen as a trade strategy, measure forward returns in out-of-sample backtests, account for delistings and corporate actions, compare to sector/market baselines, and test realistic bid-ask fills and fees. Stonk itself has no paper-trading endpoint; forward observation without orders is possible while execution remains disabled. A minute refresh changes the research display; it does not imply a profitable opportunity exists every minute. One-week research lives in the ticker-level `/api/signal` endpoint and likewise requires live current data plus historical bars.

## Data and display permissions

- [Nasdaq Trader Symbol Directory definitions](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/Trader.aspx?id=SymbolDirDefs) describe the listing files and their update timestamp.
- [Alpaca multiple-stock snapshots](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/stocksnapshots-1) documents the one-request batch endpoint and feed choices. [Alpaca's market-data FAQ](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/market-data-faq) explains that free IEX data is one venue while consolidated SIP is subscription-gated.
- A public deployment should not be given a personal Alpaca market-data key and made to redistribute its quotes anonymously. Restrict access and confirm display rights under the applicable [Alpaca market-data agreement](https://files.alpaca.markets/disclosures/library/AcctAppMarginAndCustAgmt.pdf) before enabling a live public terminal.

The app currently supports U.S. exchange-listed symbols in its directory. It does not claim complete global, OTC, or delisted stock coverage. The 50-stock home network is a curated starting view; search and ticker research are broader than that network.
